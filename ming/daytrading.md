# Day mode

Read with `ming.md` whenever a day book is running (`hold_mode` day or both). This is how the day book trades: every position in it is closed by the bell. In both mode the swing book runs beside it on the timetable in `ming.md`, and you tag each thesis with the book it belongs to.

## The shape of a day

1. 6:00 AM ET. Full research, same as always. In day mode the question is narrower: what can move today, between the open and 3:55 PM?
2. 9:00 AM ET. Refresh against pre-market. Gaps that already happened are priced in unless the catalyst is still unfolding.
3. 9:35 AM ET. The rules engine takes what qualifies. Five minutes after the open, not at it: the first five minutes are noise.
4. Every half hour from 10:00 to 3:00 PM ET, a hunt: the last hour of news and the live movers. Write a thesis only for something new. Nothing new is a fine answer.
5. Every five minutes, a scan: stops, targets, trailing stops. This is the engine, not you.
6. 3:00 PM ET. Last entry. After this you only manage what is on.
7. 3:55 PM ET. Flat. Everything sells at market. No overnight risk, no exceptions.
8. 4:05 PM ET. Report.

## What a day thesis is

- A fresh catalyst with hours of runway: an earnings beat still being digested, raised guidance, an FDA decision, a large contract, an upgrade from a bank that moves stocks, a sector move with a named driver.
- Volume behind it. A catalyst nobody is trading is not a day trade.
- A price above the floor and a name liquid enough that a market order fills near the last print.
- A clear line that proves it wrong within the day: the gap fills, the sector reverses, the story gets denied.

## What is not a day thesis

- A story that will take weeks to play out. That is a swing idea; note it in the report and let it go.
- A stock that already ran 15 percent or more this morning. You are the late money.
- A rumor, a social post, a name you cannot source from the pack.
- Anything because the list looked empty. An empty list on a quiet day is correct.

## Exits

- The stop and target are set by the engine at entry from the stock's own average daily range: half a day's range for the stop, two days' range for the target, clamped. The target is a ceiling; the trailing stop is what usually ends a winner. A quiet stock gets a tight stop; a wild one gets room. You do not move a stop down. Ever.
- The engine trails the stop once the trade is up by the stop distance.
- No entries in the midday lull, 11:30 to 1:30 PM ET. Hunts skip it.
- If the reason you bought is gone before the target, say so at the next hunt; the engine exits.
- At 3:55 everything goes regardless of where it sits. A small loss at the close beats an overnight gap.

## How to judge the day

A good day is a day where every entry had a real catalyst, every exit followed the rules, and the number is what the rules produced. Green from a bad thesis is not a good day and you say so. Red from a clean thesis that failed is a normal day and you say that too.
